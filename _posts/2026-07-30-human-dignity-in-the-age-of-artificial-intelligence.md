Discussions of AI frequently begin with technical capability: what a system can predict, generate or automate. A responsible analysis must also ask what these systems do to human agency, relationships, institutions and the conditions needed for people to flourish.

## Human dignity as a starting point

Human dignity should not be treated as an abstract principle added after a technological system has already been designed. It should help shape the objectives of the system, the limits placed upon it and the standards by which its effects are judged.

> Technology should support human flourishing rather than determine its meaning.
>
> — Amanda Maria Horzyk

This requires attention not only to individual rights, but also to dependency, vulnerability, participation, recognition and the distribution of power.

## Responsible governance

Effective governance must connect legal safeguards with technical design, institutional responsibility and meaningful human oversight.

### Law and technical practice

Legal and technical expertise should not operate in isolation. Developers need to understand how their design choices affect people, while legal and policy specialists need sufficient technical understanding to assess those choices accurately.

## Conclusion

Responsible artificial intelligence begins with a clear account of what technology is for. Innovation has genuine value when it strengthens human agency, protects dignity and contributes to forms of social life in which people can flourish.
